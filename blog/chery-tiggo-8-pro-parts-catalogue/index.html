Reading Chery part numbers

Chery part numbers are alphanumeric and usually carry the platform family in the first two characters, so two visually similar references can belong to different assemblies entirely. A number ending in a different suffix often denotes a revised version rather than a replacement: the later superseding reference is normally the one to fit, but not always, because some revisions change the mating connector or bracket.

This is why our standard practice is to confirm against the VIN before dispatch. Chinese-brand model codes also vary by market: the same body can carry different trim codes in Russia and in Brazil, and a part that fits one production batch may not fit the next.

Identifying a Tiggo 8 Pro part without the OE number

Three starting points work, in descending order of speed:

  1. The OE number itself, if the old part still carries a readable stamp.
  2. The VIN. We decode it against the Chery parts catalogue and return the applicable references.
  3. Clear photographs of the part and its markings, plus basic measurements.

Option three is the most common route for workshops dealing with a stripped or damaged original where the label is gone. It takes longer and occasionally requires a second round of photos, but it resolves the large majority of enquiries.
